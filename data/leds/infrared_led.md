# Infrared LED (IR LED)
## What it is
An Infrared LED is a diode that emits light in the infrared spectrum, which is invisible to the human eye.

## Real-world Example
The tip of your TV remote control, security cameras that can see in the dark, and the "Face ID" sensor on some smartphones.

## How it works
It works just like a normal LED, but the materials inside are chosen to produce light with a very long wavelength (around 940nm) that our eyes cannot detect, but special sensors can.

## Beginner Explanation (2nd-5th Class)
An IR LED is like a "secret flashlight." When you turn it on, it looks like it's doing nothing. But to a robot or a camera, it's glowing very brightly! It's used to send secret messages to your TV or to help robots see at night.

## Intermediate Explanation (6th-10th Class)
Because we can't see the light, it's hard to tell if an IR LED is working. **Tip:** Look at the LED through your phone's digital camera; it will usually show up as a faint purple or white glow on the screen! IR LEDs are used with IR receivers (photodiodes) to create remote controls or obstacle sensors.

## Engineering Explanation (Diploma/Engineering)
IR LEDs used in remotes are often "pulsed" with high current at a specific frequency (usually 38kHz). This allows the receiver to distinguish the remote's signal from other sources of IR light like the sun or lightbulbs. They have a very low $V_f$ (around 1.2V to 1.5V).

## Safety Tips
- Don't look directly at high-power IR LEDs for a long time. Even though you can't see the light, the energy is still there and can dry out or damage your eyes.

## Common Mistakes
- Thinking the LED is broken because it doesn't "light up."
- Forgetting that sunlight contains a lot of IR, which can interfere with your IR sensors.

## Applications
- Remote controls.
- Night vision for cameras.
- Optical "tripwires" for security.

## Simple Experiments
**Invisible Beam:** Use an IR LED and an IR receiver. If someone walks between them and "breaks" the invisible beam of light, make an alarm go off!

## FAQs
**Q: Why do security cameras have a ring of red lights?**
A: Those are usually IR LEDs that emit a tiny bit of visible red light as a side-effect, or they are "near-IR" LEDs that are slightly visible.
**Q: Can it send data?**
A: Yes! That's how your TV knows which button you pressed. It sends a pattern of fast blinks that represent the numbers for "Volume Up" or "Channel 5."
