# Coin Cell Battery (Button Cell)
## What it is
A small, flat, round battery that looks like a silver coin. The most common type is the **CR2032**.

## Real-world Example
Wristwatches, car key fobs, computer motherboards (to keep the clock running), and small "LED throwies."

## How it works
These batteries use Lithium chemistry in a very compact shape. The entire top and sides are usually the positive (+) terminal, and the small circle on the bottom is the negative (-) terminal.

## Beginner Explanation (2nd-5th Class)
A coin cell battery is like a "hidden" battery. It's so small it can fit inside a ring or a tiny toy. It doesn't have much power, but it can keep a tiny light glowing for a long, long time.

## Intermediate Explanation (6th-10th Class)
The "CR" in CR2032 stands for Lithium. The "20" means it is 20mm wide, and "32" means it is 3.2mm thick. They provide **3.0 Volts**. They are great for "Low Power" electronics that stay on for years but use almost no electricity.

## Engineering Explanation (Diploma/Engineering)
Coin cells have very high internal resistance and very low "Continuous Discharge Current" (usually only a few milliamps). They are perfect for RTC (Real Time Clock) chips and CMOS memory but cannot drive even a small motor.

## Safety Tips
- **VERY IMPORTANT:** Keep these batteries away from small children and pets. If swallowed, they can be extremely dangerous and require immediate surgery.
- Do not stack them on top of each other in a drawer; they can short-circuit and leak.

## Common Mistakes
- Trying to use a coin cell to power a bright LED for a long time. It will get dim very quickly.
- Getting fingerprints on the flat surfaces, which can increase resistance and make the battery seem "dead" prematurely.

## Applications
- Computer BIOS memory.
- Hearing aids.
- Small laser pointers.

## Simple Experiments
**LED Throwie:** Tape a small LED directly to a CR2032 battery (the long leg to the top). It will glow for days! This is the simplest circuit you can build.

## FAQs
**Q: What is the difference between CR2032 and CR2016?**
A: Both are 3V, but the 2032 is thicker and holds more energy than the 2016.
**Q: Can I recharge them?**
A: Most are not rechargeable. If the battery says "LIR" instead of "CR," it might be rechargeable.
