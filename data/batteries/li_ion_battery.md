# Li-ion Battery (Lithium-ion)
## What it is
A type of rechargeable battery in which lithium ions move from the negative electrode to the positive electrode during discharge and back when charging.

## Real-world Example
The battery inside your smartphone, laptop, and Tesla electric cars.

## How it works
Lithium-ion batteries store a lot of energy in a very small space. They use lithium as a key ingredient because it is very light and can hold a lot of electrical charge.

## Beginner Explanation (2nd-5th Class)
Li-ion batteries are like "super batteries." They can be charged up again and again, so you don't have to throw them away. They are very powerful but also very delicate, so they need a special "brain" (a small circuit) to keep them safe.

## Intermediate Explanation (6th-10th Class)
A single Li-ion cell (like the popular 18650 size) has a nominal voltage of **3.7V**. When fully charged, it is **4.2V**, and when empty, it is around **3.0V**. They are much lighter than AA batteries and can provide much more current, which is why they are used in powerful robots and drones.

## Engineering Explanation (Diploma/Engineering)
Li-ion batteries have a high energy density ($100-265 Wh/kg$). They require a Battery Management System (BMS) to prevent overcharging, over-discharging, and short circuits. Charging must follow a Constant Current / Constant Voltage (CC/CV) profile to ensure longevity and safety.

## Safety Tips
- **WARNING:** Never puncture, crush, or heat a Li-ion battery. They can catch fire or explode if damaged.
- Only use a charger specifically designed for Lithium batteries.

## Common Mistakes
- Letting the battery voltage drop too low (below 2.5V), which can permanently damage the battery so it can never be charged again.
- Using a Li-ion cell without a protection circuit (BMS).

## Applications
- Mobile phones and laptops.
- Electric vehicles.
- High-performance robots.

## FAQs
**Q: What does "18650" mean?**
A: It's the size: 18mm wide and 65.0mm long.
**Q: How many times can I recharge it?**
A: Most Li-ion batteries can be recharged between 300 and 1000 times before they start to get weak.
